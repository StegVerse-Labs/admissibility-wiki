# Three-Stage / Three-Role Observer Boundary

## Status

Public conceptual specification for governed review and reconstruction. This document does not create certification, execution authority, reviewer standing, custody, endorsement, or independent verification.

## Problem

A system may separate activity into three temporal stages without establishing three independently governed roles:

1. **Planning** — a proposal, mission plan, interpretation, or commitment candidate is prepared.
2. **Runtime** — an action is authorized, performed, observed, interrupted, or committed.
3. **After-action reconstruction** — evidence is assembled to determine what was proposed, authorized, committed, and consequential.

Temporal separation is not equivalent to authority separation. The same institution may prepare the plan, control execution, determine what runtime observers can see, preserve the evidence, and author the reconstruction.

## Core distinction

```text
three stages != three independent roles
visibility != observation authority
observation != neutral reconstruction
intervention != independent witnessing
reporting != evidence completeness
```

A runtime observer that can intervene becomes a participant in the transition it observes. An after-action reviewer remains dependent on the evidence selected, preserved, ordered, and exposed by the operating system. Therefore, neither runtime visibility nor later reporting alone establishes an independent observing function.

## Required state distinctions

A governed observer must distinguish at least four states:

```text
P = proposed state
A = authorized state
C = committed state
R = reconstructed state
```

The minimum transition record is:

```text
P -> A -> C -> R
```

These arrows must not be presumed. Each edge requires evidence sufficient to establish whether the transition occurred, who had standing, which authority applied, what changed, and whether the result can be independently reconstructed.

## Observer function

Let an interaction at time `t` produce a candidate transition `x_t`. Define an observer function:

```text
O_t(x_t, E_t, H_t, S_t) -> {PROPOSED, AUTHORIZED, COMMITTED, RECONSTRUCTED, INDETERMINATE}
```

Where:

- `E_t` is the evidence available at time `t`;
- `H_t` is the preserved historical state and ordering;
- `S_t` is the standing and authority state applicable at the boundary;
- the result classifies what is actually established rather than what a participant intended or later asserted.

The observer function is admissible only when its classification can be challenged and reconstructed from preserved evidence without inheriting authority from the proposing or executing party.

## Independence conditions

Independent observation is not established unless the system preserves:

- the proposal in the form presented before commitment;
- the applicable authorization and delegation state at commit time;
- the committed action or state mutation;
- runtime interventions and their causal effects;
- evidence origin, ordering, integrity, and omissions;
- the distinction between contemporaneous observation and later inference;
- conflicts between participant accounts;
- the reviewer’s identity, standing, scope, and conflicts;
- correction and supersession without historical erasure.

## Military operational analogue

Mission planning, runtime overwatch, and after-action reporting provide a useful operational analogue:

```text
mission planning -> proposal and authorization preparation
overwatch -> runtime observation and possible intervention
after-action report -> reconstruction and evaluation
```

The analogy becomes governance-relevant at its limitation: overwatch may alter the event, and the after-action report may be constrained by evidence controlled by the same command structure. The architecture must therefore record both stage separation and role independence.

## Human-machine mediation

A machine may preserve data relationships while altering emphasis, sequence, framing, salience, or available interpretations. Context preservation alone is therefore insufficient. The system must preserve the pathway by which an interpretation became consequential.

A proposal becomes part of shared committed reality only when the relevant boundary transition is evidenced. Meaning attributed by one participant, generated by a model, or preserved in a conversational record does not by itself establish that the meaning was mutually understood, authorized, or committed.

## Public determination rule

A public review may state only what the evidence establishes:

```text
proposal observed != authorization established
authorization asserted != authority reconstructed
action visible != committed state established
runtime monitoring != independent observation
after-action report != complete reconstruction
preserved context != shared meaning
```

When any required edge is unsupported, the correct result is `INDETERMINATE`, not an inferred transition.

## Integration targets

This concept should be referenced by:

- external-framework review templates;
- commit-time admissibility records;
- reconstruction manifests;
- reviewer-standing and conflict records;
- challenge, dissent, correction, and supersession workflows;
- runtime-governance comparisons that claim observation, oversight, or reconstructability.

## Boundary

This document specifies a review distinction. It does not claim that any current StegVerse component is an independent observer, neutral reviewer, certification authority, government-recognized body, or production execution controller.
