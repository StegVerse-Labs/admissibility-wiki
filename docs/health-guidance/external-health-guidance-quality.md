# External Health Guidance Quality Review

**Status:** active quality-improvement research  
**Posture:** recommendation guidance, not a complaint or allegation  
**Public issue:** #109

This page presents a bounded review of selected nutrition-education statements attributed to CVS Caremark / CVS Weight Management materials used in a health-and-wellness context. It contains no participant identity, member/account information, prescription details, private medical history, or individualized clinical recommendations.

Publication here does **not** establish clinical negligence, participant harm, regulatory noncompliance, certification, endorsement, or complaint disposition.

## Why this review exists

Health-and-wellness programs should be able to give participants clear nutrition education while keeping the material current, source-bound, and easy to correct when guidance changes. The findings below compare selected statements with current authoritative nutrition references.

## Findings

<!-- finding:MICRO-B6-001 -->
### Vitamin B6 reference values — confirmed discrepancy

The reviewed micronutrient guide gives adult vitamin B6 values of 1.1 mg/day for women and 1.2 mg/day for men beginning at age 19.

NIH Office of Dietary Supplements lists **1.3 mg/day for adults age 19–50**, with sex-specific values beginning at age 51.

**Recommendation:** use current age-specific vitamin B6 reference values.

<!-- finding:MICRO-K-001 -->
### Potassium reference value — outdated reference

The reviewed guide gives **4,700 mg/day** as a generalized adult potassium target.

Current NIH Office of Dietary Supplements guidance lists **3,400 mg/day for adult men and 2,600 mg/day for adult women**.

**Recommendation:** use current age/sex-specific reference values and preserve individual clinical caveats.

<!-- finding:DASH-NUTS-001 -->
### DASH nuts, seeds and legumes — confirmed discrepancy

The reviewed DASH guide says **3 servings per day**.

For the 2,000-calorie DASH pattern, NHLBI lists **4–5 servings per week**.

**Recommendation:** make DASH serving frequencies calorie-level-aware.

<!-- finding:DASH-PROTEIN-001 -->
### DASH lean meat, poultry and fish — confirmed discrepancy

The reviewed guide gives **3–6 oz of lean protein per meal**.

For the 2,000-calorie DASH pattern, NHLBI lists **6 oz or less per day** for lean meats, poultry and fish.

**Recommendation:** express this as a daily DASH total tied to calorie level rather than a per-meal range.

<!-- finding:DASH-OIL-001 -->
### DASH vegetable oils — confirmed discrepancy

The reviewed guide places vegetable oil in an avoid category.

NHLBI DASH guidance explicitly includes vegetable oils and lists **2–3 fats/oils servings per day** at the 2,000-calorie level.

**Recommendation:** distinguish unsaturated vegetable oils from saturated-fat sources rather than categorically excluding vegetable oil.

<!-- finding:PROC-INGREDIENTS-001 -->
### Five-ingredient shopping rule — oversimplified

The reviewed minimally processed foods guide recommends buying foods with five ingredients or fewer.

Ingredient count alone is not a federal measure of healthfulness or food-processing level.

**Recommendation:** teach participants to interpret ingredient composition together with the Nutrition Facts label rather than applying a hard ingredient-count cutoff.

<!-- finding:FAT-TAXONOMY-001 -->
### Saturated vs. polyunsaturated fat — confirmed discrepancy

The reviewed macronutrient guide places saturated fat in a healthy category and polyunsaturated fat in an unhealthy category.

American Heart Association guidance recommends replacing saturated and trans fats with **monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fats**.

**Recommendation:** organize the section around unsaturated fats to emphasize, saturated fats to limit, and industrial trans fats to avoid.

<!-- finding:FAT-OMEGA3-001 -->
### Omega-3 classification — confirmed discrepancy

The guide separately describes omega-3 as healthy while categorizing polyunsaturated fat as unhealthy.

Omega-3 fatty acids are themselves **polyunsaturated fats**.

**Recommendation:** nest omega-3 correctly within the polyunsaturated-fat category.

<!-- finding:FAT-OILS-001 -->
### Liquid plant oils — confirmed discrepancy

The reviewed guide categorizes corn, soybean, sunflower and safflower oils as unhealthy because of their polyunsaturated-fat content.

The American Heart Association includes these nontropical liquid plant oils among healthier choices when replacing solid/saturated fats.

**Recommendation:** do not label these oils unhealthy solely because they are polyunsaturated.

## Relationship to H2H

These external guides are useful as real-world curriculum test inputs for H2H / Heavy to Healthy. H2H does not copy them wholesale. External material enters a source-review lane, is compared with authoritative guidance, corrected where needed, and only then can become a versioned participant-education candidate under StegHealth governance.
