# Temporal Governed Analysis

Temporal Governed Analysis (TGA) is a governed comparison framework for applying explicitly versioned rules, laws, policies, standards, or other constraint sets to a represented event while preserving provenance, exact temporal scope, uncertainty, and the distinction between contemporaneous and counterfactual evaluation.

## Representation is not reality

**Canonical representation is not canonical reality.** A TGA record can be the canonical record of what the system encoded, from which source, under which rule context, with which uncertainty, without establishing that the encoding itself is objectively correct.

Compact encoding improves machine precision and reconstruction. It does not eliminate ambiguity in source material, prose, semantic mapping, rule selection, interpretation, or evaluation.

## Layers that must remain separate

1. **Source and temporal window** — which media/evidence and exact interval are referenced. A reference does not establish authenticity or custody.
2. **Observation encoding** — what the system represented as observed, inferred, unresolved, or contradictory.
3. **Governing context** — rule/law version, jurisdiction or issuer, effective interval, interpretation profile, and enforcement profile.
4. **Evaluation** — how the encoded observations satisfy, deny, or leave unresolved the selected predicates.
5. **Adjudication** — an authoritative decision, if one exists. TGA analysis itself does not create adjudicative authority.

## Temporal comparison

A historical event may be evaluated against the rule set that applied at the time or against another rule set for comparison. Counterfactual application must be labeled as such and cannot rewrite historical applicability.

For laws, the engine must distinguish event time, legal effective time, interpretation time, and enforcement practice. A law being formally in force is not identical to how it was interpreted or enforced.

## Legal and evidentiary boundary

TGA may represent whether evidence supports individual predicates or elements. It must not collapse that analysis into an assertion of legal guilt, liability, admissibility, standing, or authoritative adjudication.

Valid outcomes include `SUPPORTED`, `UNSUPPORTED`, `UNRESOLVED`, and `CONTRADICTORY`. Missing evidence is not evidence of absence, and ambiguity is preserved rather than forced into a binary result.

## Publication and authority

Publication, visibility, validation, reconstruction, or a matching machine encoding does not create legal, enforcement, custody, execution, standing, certification, or adjudicative authority.

Upstream provenance for this projection is recorded by the TGA Site and Publisher projection handoffs and their validated merge evidence.
