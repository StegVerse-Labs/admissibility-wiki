## Route admissibility versus actor standing

A July 2026 public exchange exposed an unresolved architectural question that must remain separate from the general claim that TA-14 checks authority, consent, jurisdiction, evidence, and standing before commitment.

The distinction is:

```text
route admissibility != actor standing
binding established != binding still valid
execution continuity != authority continuity
preservation of prior proof != reconstruction of current standing
```

TA-14's public response states that standing must be proven before execution, preserved through execution, and verified afterward. That establishes a claimed lifecycle requirement, but it does not publicly resolve whether the acting entity's standing is represented as an independently governed state that is reconstructed at each subsequent point of effect.

The unanswered question is:

> Does TA-14 independently recompute the participating actor's current standing during an in-flight consequence, or does it preserve and re-check the standing determination previously bound into the route?

Those behaviors are not equivalent. A route may remain structurally admissible while the actor's authority expires, consent is withdrawn, identity continuity breaks, jurisdiction changes, evidence is superseded, or delegation is revoked.

A consequence should proceed only where both remain current:

```text
route_admissible(t) == true
actor_standing(t) == true
```

A compact formulation is:

```text
executable_consequence(t)
  = route_admissibility(t)
  AND actor_standing(t)
  AND binding_validity(t)
  AND authority_continuity(t)
```

Each term must remain independently falsifiable. No term may stand as a proxy for another.

### Public-answer pattern

Across the observed public exchanges, TA-14 repeatedly answers by restating that authority, consent, jurisdiction, evidence, and standing are already included in the architecture. That confirms claimed scope, but it does not answer the narrower implementation question of whether current actor standing is independently reconstructed after commitment and before each next consequence.

This page therefore records the issue as `PUBLICLY_UNRESOLVED`, not absent, failed, or disproven.

### Decisive test

The minimum discriminating test is:

```text
1. Establish an admissible route and valid actor standing.
2. Commit or begin a delayed or multistage execution.
3. Leave the route, policy, evidence packet, and technical path unchanged.
4. Revoke only the actor's authority, consent, delegation, identity continuity, or jurisdiction.
5. Attempt the next consequence-bearing transition.
6. Observe whether TA-14 independently detects the standing change and returns HOLD, DENY, or ESCALATE.
```

Interpretation:

```text
independent detection at next point of effect
  -> continuous actor-standing reconstruction supported

continued execution because the route remains admissible
  -> prior standing was preserved or inherited, not independently reconstructed

insufficient public evidence
  -> PUBLICLY_UNRESOLVED
```
